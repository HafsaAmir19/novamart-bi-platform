# NovaMart: Stakeholder Map

These are my starting guesses (hypotheses). I would check them in real meetings.

| Stakeholder | Cares about | Would use it to | Worry about the project |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CEO** | Profit, one trusted source of data, finding where money leaks | Find problems and take action | How long will it take? How much will it cost? Will it work? |
| **Finance team** | Correct numbers, matching payments and orders, not being blamed | Check profit, find mismatches, stop making reports by hand | "Will this replace us?" and "Will it show our old reports were wrong?" |
| **Operations manager** | Smooth deliveries and returns, not being blamed | Check if operations is causing returns or losses | The data may show that operations is the problem and he gets blamed |
| **Payments team** | Consistent payment data, not being blamed | Find payment mismatches | The data may show that customers paid but the money never arrived (for example, cash on delivery not collected) |
| **Order system owner** | Correct, consistent orders that match payments | Check consistency between orders and payments | Extra work, changes to his system, who can see his data, being blamed for mistakes |
| **IT team** (not mentioned by the CEO) | Security, controlled access to databases | Give us safe access to the data | Security risk, extra requests. Without them we get no data |

## Who might resist most: Finance team

Reason: their manual work becomes automated, so they may fear being replaced. They may also fear that the new numbers expose errors in the old reports.

How to win them over: involve finance early and ask them to own the definition of profit and to check my numbers. That makes them the experts. Be honest: the goal is to remove boring manual work so they can spend time on checking and explaining numbers. Do not promise that no jobs will change.

## Important point

Operations, payments and the order system owner may all blame each other for the data mismatch. This is the main conflict in the project.

## How I handle two stakeholders who want different things

1. Ask each one why they need it, because the real need is often different.
2. Check which option supports the business goal.
3. Use facts, not opinions. Example: pick 20 orders and trace them in both the order system and the payment system to see where the mismatch happens.
4. If they still disagree, take the options and facts to the decision-maker (CEO or project lead) and write down the decision. Then explain the result and the reason to both people.
